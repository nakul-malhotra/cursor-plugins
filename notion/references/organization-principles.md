# Organization and content review

Give a document a canonical home that matches its purpose, with links from other useful contexts. A task may reference its deliverable without being the deliverable's permanent home.

Use pages for narratives and decisions. Use databases when repeated records need shared properties, filtering, or multiple views. Existing healthy organization takes precedence over a generic preference for databases or shallow nesting.

Review structural and content problems separately. A structural finding identifies an actual parent, intended destination, and evidence. A content finding identifies a contradiction, unsupported claim, missing rationale, obsolete instruction, or unclear action. Verify factual claims against their source before rewriting.

Duplicate titles suggest investigation; they do not identify duplicates. A 404 can mean missing permission. A sparse page can be an intentional stub. Completed documents must not be turned back into pending work.

Report each finding with its page ID/link, evidence, confidence, impact, proposed change, and approval needs. Report coverage and access gaps. Avoid arbitrary quality scores or universal rules such as a fixed maximum number of nesting levels.
